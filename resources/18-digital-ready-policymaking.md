# 18. Digital-Ready Policymaking - ADAPTED, no obligation attaches

Source: Interoperable Europe Portal, "Digital-Ready Policymaking" collection.
<https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/digital-ready-policymaking>
Retrieved 2026-08-08. Publisher: European Commission, DG DIGIT.

## The definition that governs

> "Digital-ready policymaking (DRPM) is the process of formulating digital-ready
> policies and legislation by taking digital aspects into account from the start
> of the policy cycle."

Its object is the *drafting* of policy. This project's object is the
*encoding* of policy already issued. Those are opposite ends of the same
lifecycle, and conflating them is the error to avoid when citing this source.

## Why no obligation attaches

DRPM sits under Regulation (EU) 2024/903, the Interoperable Europe Act, adopted
13 March 2024. That regulation binds Union institutions and Member State public
sector bodies. It has no extraterritorial reach into a US Department of Defense
component, and no DoD issuance incorporates it by reference. Nothing here is a
requirement. It is a method, and the method is good.

The specific mechanism the Act adds - a mandatory interoperability assessment
before adopting requirements that affect cross-border digital public services -
is the part worth reading. Its exact commencement date and scope thresholds are
**UNCONFIRMED**: EUR-Lex returned only page furniture to automated retrieval on
2026-08-08. Anyone quoting the assessment obligation in a deliverable must pull
the article text from CELEX 32024R0903 directly.

| DRPM Framework | the method documentation | named, not retrieved |
| ICT Impact Assessment Guidelines | evaluating technology implications of a draft | named, not retrieved |
| LIMAPS | legal interoperability self-assessment tool | named, not retrieved |
| Decision Support Tool | interoperability considerations during drafting | named, not retrieved |
| Better Regulation Toolbox, Tool 28 | the drafting checklist the framework leans on | referenced by the portal, not detailed there |

The collection also carries a Knowledge Centre, a Best Practices repository, and
a glossary. Community size at retrieval: 288 members, 2 catalogued solutions.
That second number is the one to notice. A portal with 288 members and 2
solutions is a knowledge hub, not an implementation base.

## The claim to handle carefully

The portal states Denmark's e-invoicing initiative "saves taxpayers €150 million
and businesses €50 million each year," and extrapolates "over €50 billion in
yearly savings" EU-wide. The Danish figure is long-standing and traceable. The
€50 billion extrapolation is a portal claim with no method shown. **Do not
carry it into any deliverable.** If a savings argument is needed, build it from
the corpus this project holds, not from a linear scale-up of one Member State's
invoicing programme.

## What transfers

**The upstream argument.** DRPM's core insight is that machine-readability is
cheap when designed in at drafting and expensive when retrofitted. This
repository is the retrofit case, and it can quantify the cost: hand-encoding to
VERIFIED tier is why `data/` holds a handful of documents while
`data/exports/` holds 56 machine-produced ones stamped UNVERIFIED. That ratio is
the DRPM argument stated in evidence rather than in principle.

**The direction of travel for SemperScribe.** The authoring side named in
conformance matrix section 10 is where DRPM bites. A drafting tool that
emits structured provisions at authoring time removes the extraction step
entirely. That is the DRPM thesis, applied to Marine Corps issuances. This is
the strongest reason to keep this source in the register.

## What does not transfer

The legal-interoperability instruments assume a multi-jurisdiction, multi-language
setting where semantic barriers arise between Member States. The corpus here is
single-jurisdiction and single-language, with hierarchy rather than
peer-negotiation as its structuring problem. LIMAPS and the ICT Impact
Assessment Guidelines have no purchase on a MARADMIN.

## Verdict

**ADAPTED, no obligation attaches.** The method informs the authoring side and
supplies the argument for why this project exists. The legal instrument binds
nothing here, and no deliverable should imply otherwise.

p-2 · 2. Bullying. A form of harassment that includes acts of

2. Bullying. A form of harassment that includes acts of aggression by Service Members or DON civilian employees, with a nexus to military service, with the intent of harming a Service Members or DON civilian employees either physically or psychologically, without a proper military or other governmental purpose. Bullying may involve the singling out of an individual from coworkers, or unit, for ridicule because the individual is considered different or weak. It often involves an imbalance of power between the aggressor and the victim. Bullying can be conducted through the use of electronic devices, including social media, other forms of communication, as well as in person. Bullying is evaluated by a reasonable person standard.
a. Bullying includes, but is not limited to the following when performed without a proper military or other governmental purpose:
(1) Physically striking another person in any manner or threatening to do the same.
(2) Intimidating, teasing, or taunting another person.
(3) Oral or written berating of another person with the purpose of belittling or humiliating.
(4) Encouraging another person to engage in illegal, harmful, demeaning, or dangerous acts.
(5) Playing abusive or malicious tricks.
(6) Branding, handcuffing, duct taping, tattooing, shaving, greasing, or painting another person.
(7) Subjecting another person to excessive or abusive use of water. SECNAVINST 1610.3A 13 May 2026 2 Enclosure (2)
(8) Forcing another person to consume food, alcohol, drugs, or any other substance.
(9) Degrading or damaging another’s property or reputation.
(10) Soliciting, coercing, or knowingly permitting another person to solicit or coerce acts of bullying.
b. Bullying does not include properly directed command or organizational activities that serve a proper military or other governmental purpose, or the requisite training activities required to prepare for such activities (e.g., command- authorized physical training).
c. Service Members or DoW civilian employees may be responsible for an act of bullying even if there was actual or implied consent from the victim and regardless of the grade or rank, status, or Service of the victim.
d. Bullying is prohibited in all circumstances and environments, including off-duty or “unofficial” unit functions and settings.

5. Cyberharassment. A form of harassment, as defined herein, or sexual harassment, as defined in reference (b), occurring through or facilitated by an electronic communications system. Cyberharassment may include, but is not limited to, threatening, derogatory, or harassing text or email messages, instant messages, social media comments, blog entries, or similar communications. The broadcast or public sharing via an SECNAVINST 1610.3A 13 May 2026 3 Enclosure (2) electronic communications system of non-publicly available information about an individual, also referred to as doxing, can be a form of cyberharassment.
6. Discriminatory Harassment. A form of harassment that is unwelcome conduct based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex (including pregnancy), or sexual orientation.

p-9 · 9. Hazing. A form of harassment that includes conduct through

9. Hazing. A form of harassment that includes conduct through which Service Members or DON civilian employees, without a proper military or other governmental purpose, but with a nexus to military service, physically or psychologically injures or creates a risk of physical or psychological injury to Service Members or DON civilian employees for the purpose of: initiation into, admission into, affiliation with, change in status or position within, or a condition for continued membership in any military or DoW civilian organization. Hazing can be conducted through the use of electronic devices, including social media, other forms of communication, as well as in person.
a. Hazing is evaluated by a reasonable person standard and includes, but is not limited to, the following when performed without a proper military or other governmental purpose:
(1) Any form of initiation or congratulatory act that involves physically striking another person in any manner or threatening to do the same.
(2) Pressing any object into another person’s skin, regardless of whether it pierces the skin, such as “pinning” or “tacking on” of rank insignia, aviator wings, jump wings, diver insignia, badges, medals, or any other object. SECNAVINST 1610.3A 13 May 2026 4 Enclosure (2)
(3) Oral or written berating of another person with the purpose of belittling or humiliating.
(4) Encouraging another person to engage in illegal, harmful, demeaning, or dangerous acts.
(5) Playing abusive or malicious tricks.
(6) Branding, handcuffing, duct taping, tattooing, shaving, greasing, or painting another person.
(7) Subjecting another person to excessive or abusive use of water.
(8) Forcing another person to consume food, alcohol, drugs, or any other substance.
(9) Soliciting, coercing, or knowingly permitting another person to solicit or coerce acts of hazing.
b. Hazing does not include properly directed command or organizational activities that serve a proper military or other governmental purpose, or the requisite training activities required to prepare for such activities (e.g., administrative corrective measures, extra military instruction, or command- authorized physical training).
c. Service Members or DON civilian employees may be responsible for an act of hazing even if there was actual or implied consent from the victim and regardless of the grade or rank, status, or Service of the victim.
d. Hazing is prohibited in all circumstances and environments including off-duty or “unofficial” unit functions and settings.

p-1-3 · 1. Harassment. Harassment is contrary to our Core Values of

1. Harassment. Harassment is contrary to our Core Values of Honor, Courage, and Commitment. Adherence to Core Values by our Sailors and Marines is central to the DON’s ability to meet its global mission. These values have served the Sea Services well during war and peacetime. They are our guiding principles for treating every Sailor and Marine with dignity and respect, and as a valued member of the Navy and Marine Corps team. Harassment degrades and diminishes the ability of a Sailor or Marine to function within their unit. It erodes our members' confidence and trust in their shipmates and is destructive to unit cohesion and combat readiness. Every member of DON will be afforded the opportunity to be a productive and contributing member, free of harassment and its ill effects.
a. DON leadership has a responsibility to create and maintain an environment free from harassment.
b. Harassment is prohibited and will not be tolerated by any member of the Navy or Marine Corps.
c. No Service Member in the DON may engage in harassment or consent to acts of harassment being committed upon them.
d. No commander or supervisor may, by act, word, deed, or omission, condone or ignore harassment if the commander or supervisor knows or reasonably should have known that harassment may or did occur.
e. Every Service Member has the responsibility to report each violation of this policy to leadership or appropriate authorities. Commanders and supervisors must commence, or cause the commencement of, an investigation of a formal complaint of harassment within five duty days of receipt of the complaint using the procedures listed in reference (a).

p-2-2 · 2. Hazing. Military customs and traditions have long been an

2. Hazing. Military customs and traditions have long been an integral part of the Navy and Marine Corps. These activities, if properly supervised, can be effective leadership tools to instill esprit de corps, unit cohesion, and respect for an accomplishment of another Sailor or Marine. While most ceremonies commemorate the many selfless acts of bravery of our military, they also commemorate significant events. These SECNAVINST 1610.3A 13 May 2026 2 Enclosure (3) actions and events form the basis upon which our Core Values of Honor, Courage, and Commitment were founded. Graduations, chiefs' initiations, "crossing-the-line" ceremonies, and others are meant to celebrate and recognize the achievements of individual Sailors or Marines or those of entire units. Service Members must be able to work together, build-up, encourage, and support their shipmates. Incorporating or condoning behavior that is degrading, embarrassing, abusive, or injurious as part of our ceremonies, initiations, or rites of passage can be illegal and unprofessional. Hazing is not part of our "time honored traditions" and has no place in the modern Naval Services.
a. Commanders or supervisors must be aware of all ceremonies and initiations conducted within their organizations and must take active measures to ensure that these activities do not violate this policy.
b. Commanders or supervisors must ensure that Service Members participating in command authorized ceremonies, initiations, and other activities are treated with dignity and respect during these events.
3. Complaints of sexual harassment may be reported pursuant to reference (b) and processed using the procedures outlined in references (b) and (c).
4. Retaliation. Retaliation or reprisal actions, as defined in this instruction, enclosure (2), against any victim or witness of harassment incidents are strictly prohibited.

p-5-2 · 5. Applicability. The rules in this instruction, enclosure

5. Applicability. The rules in this instruction, enclosure (3), paragraph 5.a. are regulatory orders and apply to all DON military personnel individually and without further implementation. A violation of these provisions by military personnel may be punishable pursuant to reference (k). The prohibitions in this instruction, enclosure (3), paragraph 5.a. apply to all conduct which occurs in or affects a DoW working environment, or otherwise meets the definition of harassment, cyberharassment, discriminatory harassment, hazing, bullying, or stalking as defined in this instruction, enclosure (2). SECNAVINST 1610.3A 13 May 2026 3 Enclosure (3)
a. DON military personnel will not intentionally or recklessly:
(1) Commit harassment, as defined in this instruction, enclosure (2).
(2) Retaliate against a person who provides information on an incident of alleged harassment, cyberharassment, discriminatory harassment, hazing, bullying or stalking, obstruct an investigation of such an incident, or act to prevent a victim or witness from reporting harassment, cyberharassment, discriminatory harassment, hazing, bullying, or stalking.
(3) Make a false accusation of harassment, cyberharassment, discriminatory harassment, hazing, bullying, or stalking.
(4) While in a supervisory or command position, condone or ignore harassment, cyberharassment, discriminatory harassment, hazing, bullying, or stalking of which he or she has knowledge or reasonably should have knowledge.
b. The appropriate action to resolve a substantiated incident of harassment will depend upon the circumstances surrounding that incident. Incidents of harassment cover a wide range of behaviors, from verbal comments to physical acts, and can be subtle or overt. Likewise, the full range of administrative and disciplinary actions are available to address harassment.
(1) In the case of military personnel, these include, but are not limited to, informal counseling, comments in fitness reports and evaluations, administrative separation, and punitive measures under reference (k).
(2) Substantiated complaints will be annotated on fitness reports, evaluations, or Service-level reporting and tracking system(s). The Service-level tracking system shall be reviewed for substantiated harassment incidents prior to a Service member’s selection for promotion and other favorable personnel actions.

p-6 · 6. Leadership. Sound leadership must be the cornerstone of the

No provision hierarchy was parsed for this section. Its source text is shown as extracted, unaltered.

6. Leadership. Sound leadership must be the cornerstone of the effort to eliminate harassment. It is not the intent of this SECNAVINST 1610.3A 13 May 2026 4 Enclosure (3) instruction to impair commanders and supervisors in their ability to take appropriate actions to carry out leadership responsibilities. They must set the example in treating all people with dignity and respect, and fostering a climate free of harassment. Such a climate is essential to maintain high morale, discipline, and readiness. Commanders and supervisors are responsible for and must be committed to preventing harassment in their commands and work environments. They must not ignore or condone harassment in any form, and they must take whatever action is required to ensure that a victim of harassment is not subsequently also the victim of retaliation or reprisal. Retaliation or reprisal, as further described in this instruction, enclosure (2); and reference (a), is prohibited under this instruction whether it is originated by the command, the alleged perpetrator, or peers of the complainant. These responsibilities regarding harassment are part of the broader responsibility of commanders and supervisors to foster a positive climate and take appropriate corrective action when conduct is disruptive, provoking, discriminatory, or otherwise unprofessional.

Does the order say what its authority says?

Marine Corps orders cite the DoD instructions and statutes they implement. Nobody can check today, at scale, whether a citation still points at something in force, or whether the number in the order matches the number in the authority above it. This site is a proof that both checks can be made mechanical. 56 documents, 20,178 paragraphs, every claim traceable to the paragraph that states it.

One question, walked end to end

Does MARADMIN 051/23 give a Marine the parental leave the DoD instruction authorizes?

  1. The message. MARADMIN 051/23, paragraph 11.d, states 84 days. Read the paragraph
  2. Where it sits. Statute, DoD instruction, Marine Corps order, message. Every step is a citation printed on the document below it, and a tier the corpus does not hold is stated as a gap, never inferred. The chain
  3. The authority. DoD Instruction 1327.06, paragraph 3.11.c.(3)(a)1, states twelve weeks. As read and confirmed
  4. Do they agree. Yes. The DoD instruction states 12 weeks; the message states 84 days. Both come to 84 days. The comparison
  5. Who confirmed it. 3 of 11 encoded values have been read against the issuing authority's copy by a named person. 4 of 5 comparisons are withheld until both sides have been read. Withheld, not guessed. The ledger
  6. What follows. Three fixed cases run through the encoded rules: days remaining, the forfeiture date, whether a proposed block is valid. Every line cites its paragraph; a line resting on a value nobody has confirmed is withheld and says which. The scenarios
  7. What this proves, and what it does not. It compares encoded values, never the issuances themselves. The issuing authority's copy governs. Where a value has not been read by a person, the page says so instead of showing a number. A wrong entitlement figure with a caveat under it is worse than no figure.
